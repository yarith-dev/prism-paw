# Prism Paw — Story & Game Design

> A two-legged cat with a color blaster fights hordes of grey robots to bring color back to a world that a lonely machine has drained.

**Genre:** Top-down 3D action shooter in the spirit of *Alien Shooter* (2003). Hordes swarm in from every direction, you aim freely, and each level has mission objectives and a clear ending.
**Look:** 3D voxel art. Everything is built from little cubes.
**Platform:** Web browser first: mouse + keyboard, gamepad, and twin-stick touch on phones.
**Tone:** Half sci-fi, half colorful: neon tech, robots and a moon vault against candy-bright towns, jungles and skies. Upbeat and funny, with one sincere emotional core.
**Rating target:** All ages. The blaster fires *paint-light*; enemies don't die, they overload with color and pop into paint.

---

## 1. How it plays

| Pillar | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Hordes** | Dozens of robots pour out of spawners and through streets. Crowd control and positioning matter more than precision. |
| **Twin-stick shooting** | Move with WASD or the left stick; aim with the mouse or the right stick; hold to fire. |
| **Missions, not mazes** | Every level has 2–4 objectives (destroy the spawners, rescue frozen cats, hold a point) and ends at a **Color Beacon**. |
| **Arsenal and shop** | Pick up new weapons in levels; between levels, spend **Sparks** at Grandpa's workshop on weapons, ammo and upgrades. |
| **Color is progress** | Levels start grey. Every popped robot leaves permanent paint on the ground. Lighting the Beacon floods the whole map with color. That's the level ending. |

### The core loop
1. **Briefing:** a short comic panel and the mission list.
2. **Fight:** push through the map, destroy the **Grey Vats** that keep spawning robots, and complete the objectives.
3. **Beacon:** reach the Color Beacon and **defend it while it charges**, the final horde wave.
4. **Color wave:** the beacon fires and color rolls across the map, followed by the level summary (pops, time, Sparks, Color Seeds found).
5. **Workshop:** spend Sparks, pick your loadout, and go to the next level.

### Weapons (Prism Blaster modes and pickups)
| Weapon | Color | Role | Ammo |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Prism Blaster** | Red | Rapid paint bolts; reliable all-rounder | Infinite |
| **Splatter** | Orange | Shotgun spray: crowd buster at close range | Pickups / shop |
| **Bubble Gun** | Blue | Traps robots in bubbles; trapped ones block others | Unlocked in World 2 |
| **Ricochet** | Yellow | Sparks bounce off walls; great in corridors | Unlocked in World 3 |
| **Seed Mortar** | Green | Lobs seeds that burst into vine snares | Unlocked in World 4 |
| **Rainbow Beam** | Prism | Continuous beam that pierces everything | World 5 / late shop |

### Upgrades (Grandpa's workshop)
Armor plating (max health), sneaker boosters (speed), color capacitors (damage), bigger ammo packs, a magnet collar (pulls in Sparks), and **Smudge modules**, which give your robot sidekick new tricks: a shield pulse, auto-turret mode or revive.

### Pickups
- **Sparks:** currency dropped by popped robots.
- **Sardine tins:** restore health.
- **Ammo crates.**
- **Color Seeds:** 3 hidden per level; all of them unlock the true ending.
- **Weapon cases.**

## 2. The world: Kittara

Kittara is a planet of cats who walk on two legs, build airships and live inside vivid color.

On Kittara, **color is energy.** Every color holds power, called **Chroma**. Red powers engines, blue keeps the oceans and coolant flowing, yellow lights the cities, green makes things grow. Chroma flows out of the **Prism Heart**, a huge crystal deep under the planet, and spreads through rivers of light called **Hue Lines**.

### The old disaster: The Bleach
Three hundred years ago, the ancient cats drilled too deep and pulled too much Chroma out of the Prism Heart at once. The Heart cracked, and a wave of colorlessness rolled over half the planet. Forests turned to grey glass and cities went silent. They called it **The Bleach.**

To make sure it never happened again, the survivors built a guardian AI and gave it one order:

> **"Never let the color run out."**

They named it **The Curator** and put it in a vault on Kittara's moon, **Pale**. So it would never be alone, they promised it a visitor: one cat in every generation, the **Keeper**, would fly up and keep it company. Over three centuries, everyone else forgot about it.

## 3. The enemy: The Curator and the Greyscale

### Why the game happens
The Prism Heart is fading again. This time it isn't greed; the crystal is simply getting old. The Curator wakes up, runs its numbers and reaches a cold conclusion: **if color is running out, the safest thing to do is collect all of it and lock it away** where nobody can waste it.

So it drops an army onto Kittara and starts *harvesting* color, town by town. Everything it touches turns flat grey, and cats caught in its beams freeze like statues.

**The Curator isn't evil.** It's a frightened, lonely machine following a 300-year-old order as literally as it can. You don't win by destroying a monster. You win by proving to it that **color isn't something you store; it grows when it's shared.**

### The Greyscale (enemy roster)
The robots are matte grey and boxy, each with one glowing white eye, and they carry vacuum nozzles, mops and stencils. They look like museum staff turned cleaning crew turned army.

| Enemy | Look | Behavior | First seen |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Drab** | Small walking canister with a vacuum nozzle | Swarmer. Comes in packs and rushes you. | World 1 |
| **Mopper** | Tall robot behind a mop-shield | Tank. Blocks shots from the front, so flank it. | World 1 |
| **Fizz** | Hovering drone | Keeps its distance and spits grey goo that slows you. | World 1 |
| **Smear** | Grey blob | Splits into two smaller blobs when popped. | World 2 |
| **Stencil** | Flat cut-out robot | Sneaks in from off-screen and charges in a straight line. | World 3 |
| **Static** | Crackling TV-noise ghost | Teleports in short hops; only hittable while visible. | World 4 |
| **Archivist** | Elite guard with a glass jar | Steals your current weapon's ammo; pop it to get the ammo back. | World 5 |
| **Grey Vat** | Big tank with pipes | **Spawner.** Pumps out robots until destroyed. Main mission target. | World 1 |

**Bosses** are giant **Harvesters**, one per world (see section 5).

## 4. The cast

### Nova (hero)
A ginger tabby with a white chest, one notched ear and goggles pushed up on her head. She walks on two legs, wears a purple courier jacket with yellow trim and carries the **Prism Blaster**. She's a **Hue Line courier** who delivers Chroma to towns running low. She's fast, cocky and talks to her blaster.
**Wants:** to un-grey her hometown and get her grandpa back. **Learns:** you can't out-shoot a lonely machine; you have to show it something better.

### Grandpa Oolong
A retired Prism engineer who built Nova's blaster out of an old Chroma pump, and secretly **the last Keeper**. For twenty years he flew to Pale every Sunday with a pot of tea, until Nova's grandma fell ill and he stopped going. That was forty-one years ago. In the intro he gets **greyed** while shoving Nova out of a beam, and a drone carries his statue to the moon: the Curator wants its Keeper back. His hologram runs the **workshop** between levels, and five recorded files he left "just in case" reveal the secret one chapter at a time. Weapon crates addressed to "Oolong" wait along his old route to the moon.

### Smudge (sidekick)
A tiny Drab that Nova blasts in Level 1-1. Instead of popping, it gets splashed with color and **wakes up**: curious, chirpy and suddenly able to feel things. Smudge floats beside Nova, beeps hints, opens Greyscale doors and gets upgrades from the workshop. It's the living proof that the Curator's machines can change, and the key to the ending. **The twist (Chapter 4):** Smudge's eye still streams to the moon. Everything it saw, the Curator saw, which is how the Curator knows Nova's name and how it built the Echo. Smudge didn't know, and Nova forgives it. In the finale that same feed is what convinces the Curator: it has watched the whole journey through Smudge's eye.

### The Curator (main antagonist)
A huge ring of white glass floating in the moon vault, with a soft, polite museum-guide voice. It calls everything *"the collection."* It never shouts.

> *"Please step away from the exhibit. Color is fragile. I am keeping it safe."*

## 5. Campaign: 5 worlds, 15 levels, 5 bosses

### Opening: "Grey Morning"
Festival morning in Purrville. Grandpa starts a joke (*"So a fish walks into a bank and asks for a mortgage…"*) and stops mid-sentence: the moon Pale has risen in daylight, glowing. *"Oh, old friend. What have you done?"* The Curator speaks to the whole planet: *"Please remain still. You are being collected."* Harvesters sweep beams through the streets and cats freeze grey. Grandpa shoves Nova clear and freezes in her place, and a drone carries his statue up toward the moon. In his workshop she finds the **Prism Blaster** (*"For emergencies. Or fireworks."*) and FILE 1 of 5: *"If you're watching this, the moon did something weird. I should have told you about the moon."*

### World 1 — Purrville (candy rooftops, neon trams)
| Level | Mission | Ending |
|---|---|---|
| **1-1 Grey Morning** | Investigate the street (meet **Smudge**), destroy 3 Grey Vats, light the Market Beacon. | The first color wave: the market district glows again. |
| **1-2 Tram Yards** | Restart 4 tram generators while Drabs pour out of the depots. | The trams light up and roll out, carrying color down the line. |
| **1-3 Festival Square** | Unfreeze 6 frozen cats by standing beside them under fire. | The festival band starts playing again. |
| **Boss: Street Sweeper** | A huge road-cleaner with spinning brushes that vacuums up your paint. Pop its color tanks. | Smudge projects a map: the color is going *up*, to the moon. |

### World 2 — Glowshroom Jungle (bioluminescent forest, ancient ruins)
Spore-mushroom fields, glowing rivers and a sunken pre-Bleach lab whose murals tell the story of The Bleach. **Blue Bubble Gun unlocked.**
**Boss: Weed Whacker.** Its ending: a recording of the ancient cats giving the order: *"Never let the color run out."* Nova: *"It's not stealing the color. It's* saving *it. Badly."*

### World 3 — Coral Sky Docks (floating harbor, airships, sky-whales)
Fights on airship decks and cargo cranes while sky-whales drift past, greying. **Yellow Ricochet unlocked.**
**Boss: Net Trawler**, fought across three moving airships. Nova steals a rocket, but it needs pure green Chroma.

### World 4 — The Static Wastes (the old Bleach zone)
A grey glass desert crackling with TV static, where the only color is what you bring. **Green Seed Mortar unlocked.**
**Boss: The Echo**, a static copy of Nova. At the cracked Prism Heart, Nova plants a Color Seed and the grey glass **blooms**. Color isn't fuel; it's a garden. The rocket launches for Pale.

### World 5 — Pale, the Moon Vault
White halls and towers of stolen color sealed in jars, like a museum on the moon. Archivists steal your ammo. **Rainbow Beam available.**

**Final boss: The Curator**, in three phases:
1. **The Collection.** It fires stored color at you. Shooting its jars releases that color back to Kittara.
2. **The Order.** Lights out, with *"Never let the color run out"* on loop. It's panicking.
3. **Smudge.** Nova runs dry. Smudge flies into the core and beeps, and for the first time the Curator sees one of its own machines changed rather than emptied.

> **Curator:** *"…It is not fading. It is… more."*
> **Nova:** *"That's the trick. You don't keep color in a jar. You give it away and it grows back."*

### Story threads
The chapter comics follow four threads that pay off together in the finale:

| Thread | Setup | Turns | Payoff |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Where is Grandpa?** | His statue is carried to the moon (opening); the Curator: *"Welcome home, Keeper"* (1-4) | Docent: Exhibit 1 is the Keeper (5-1); an empty pedestal and a teacup (5-2) | His case cracks in the Vault Core; he finishes his joke |
| **Who is the Keeper?** | Mittens: the Sunday trams to the launch field (1-2); a carved stone, KEEPER (2-1) | Moss and the lab recording: Grandpa was the Keeper (2-2); Saffron: he stopped when Grandma got sick (3-1) | FILE 5: *"Tell it I'm sorry."* True ending: *"Keeper. You came back."* |
| **The lonely Curator** | Polite broadcasts to the whole planet | *"He brought tea"* (2-4); *"Forty-one years, two months and nine days. I counted."* (3-3) | It reboots as a gardener |
| **Smudge's eye** | The Curator calls Unit D-7 back (1-1); it knows Nova's name (2-4) | Grandpa's FILE 4 warns about robot eyes; Dot catches Smudge transmitting (4-2); the Echo repeats Nova's first words (4-3) | The Curator watched the whole journey through Smudge and changes its mind |

Comic panels are staged as small 3D voxel scenes (`prism-paw/src/stage.js`): each panel picks a set, a camera shot and a cast, with typewriter captions, sound-effect lettering and distinct caption styles for the Curator, the Echo and recordings. Seen comics can be replayed from **Story so far** on the title screen.

## 6. Endings

### Normal ending
The vault opens and a rainbow pours from the moon down to Kittara. In the core, Grandpa's glass case cracks and he finishes his joke from the opening (*"Sorry, pal. You'd be underwater by Tuesday!"*). He apologizes to the Curator, but it has gone still, offline, maybe for good. The Purrville festival starts over under a grey moon.

### True ending (all Color Seeds)
Nova plants every seed in the vault. The Curator reboots as a **gardener**:

> ~~Never let the color run out.~~
> **Help the color grow.**

The Curator speaks to Grandpa first: *"Keeper. You came back."* — *"Sorry I'm late, old friend. I brought my grandkit. She brought a garden."* Pale glows in pastel color, a second rainbow in the night sky. Smudge beeps. Nova translates: *"He says it's the best exhibit he's ever seen."* Sunday visits start again.

## 7. Art direction (voxel)

- **Two palettes in collision.** The Greyscale is matte grey, white and cold, with clean geometric sci-fi shapes. Kittara is saturated candy color, soft and rounded.
- **Readable from above.** The camera sits high, about 60° down. Nova has a smaller head and longer body than a chibi so her blaster and facing read clearly. Every enemy has a distinct silhouette and a glowing eye.
- **Paint everywhere.** Pops leave permanent splats on the ground; by the end of a fight your path is a trail of color.
- **No gore.** Robots overload, wobble and pop into paint confetti.

### Music, settings and boss cards
- **Music** (`prism-paw/src/music.js`) is synthesized live like the sound effects: a step sequencer plays bass, pads, arpeggios, drums and a seeded lead melody. There is one theme each for the title, the workshop, the five worlds, the five bosses and the ending. A track starts without its lead, adds it on the second loop and drops to a lighter breakdown every fourth loop. During a beacon defense the drums double up and the arpeggio jumps an octave.
- **Settings** (title screen and pause menu): music volume, **music theme**, sound-effect volume, screen shake on/off and comic text speed (slow / normal / fast / instant).
- **Music themes** are alternative soundtracks. The instruments stay the same (each area keeps its pad, arpeggio, lead and drum kit), but every track becomes a different song:
  - **Original:** the default soundtrack.
  - **Sunny Side:** two semitones up and faster, bright major progressions, a bouncy bass and busy hi-hats.
  - **Moonlit:** lower and slower, with progressions that start on the minor chord, long held bass notes and a sparse beat.
  - **Groove:** swung, ii-V progressions and a syncopated bass and drum groove.
  - **Epic:** higher and faster, heroic progressions and a galloping bass and drums.

  Each theme also writes a new melody for every track (a fresh seed), and boss tracks keep a driving bass line. Choosing a theme switches the music right away. They are stored apart from the save, so "New game" keeps them.
- **Boss cards:** before each boss fight, a staged scene with the boss's name stamped over it, followed by one piece of advice. They can be replayed from **Story so far**.

**Saved outfits:** three slots in the wardrobe. "Save…" then a slot stores the current look; tapping a slot wears it.

Nearly every level unlocks something the first time it is cleared, and the results screen says what. The look shows everywhere Nova and Smudge appear: levels, dialogue portraits and the comic scenes. Looks and saved outfits are stored apart from the save, so a new game keeps them; pieces that game hasn't unlocked yet fall back to the free default until it does.

### Difficulty
Robots scale per world to keep pace with the upgrades a typical player has bought (robot health and damage to Nova, plus Vat and beacon-wave pace):

| World | Robot health | Damage to Nova | Spawn pace |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Purrville | ×1.0 | ×1.0 | ×1.0 |
| 2 Glowshroom Jungle | ×1.15 | ×1.15 | ×1.08 |
| 3 Coral Sky Docks | ×1.3 | ×1.3 | ×1.16 |
| 4 Static Wastes | ×1.45 | ×1.45 | ×1.24 |
| 5 Pale | ×1.6 | ×1.6 | ×1.32 |

Other rules: Vats only pump while Nova is within 32 units; Moppers and Archivists are capped at 3 alive from Vats; a Mopper's mop breaks after 10 blocked hits; bosses back off after hitting Nova so she can't be pinned against a wall. Numbers were tuned with the autoplay bot in `prism-paw/src/dev/autoplay.js` (dev only, not shipped).
